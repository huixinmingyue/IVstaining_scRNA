Fig. 6 | Vascular-contact CNS immune cells rewire original outgoing signalling and myeloid programmes.

a, Flow-cytometric gates used to separate parenchyma-associated (NEG, 58.3%) from vascular-contact (POS, 15.4%) CNS immune cells. Extra CD45 is the intravenous surface label; Intra CD45 is the intracellular stain. Gate percentages are the native cytometer statistics on the plot.
b, UMAP of CNS immune cells split by POS and NEG, coloured by cell type (n = 15,792 POS; 31,184 NEG). All cells are plotted; point marks are rasterised at 600 dpi with vector axes.
c, Cell-type proportions within each group.
d, CellChat outgoing POS/NEG ratio for the ten pathways with the highest total outgoing strength (GALECTIN to CSF), computed from the original (not composition-matched) matrices. The x axis is log10-scaled; the vertical line marks a ratio of 1. Bars use the same POS/NEG colours as c (POS, ratio >= 1; NEG, ratio < 1).
e, KEGG over-representation of macrophage POS-versus-NEG DEGs (padj < 0.001 and |log2 FC| >= 1 after removal of Rpl/Rps, mt and Gm genes), restricted to signalling-pathway terms. Over-representation was tested against all 218 KEGG terms; the ten most significant signalling terms are shown. BH-adjusted P values are from the full test. No macrophage signalling term reaches FDR < 0.05 (lowest FDR = 0.42), so panel e is exploratory.
f, KEGG over-representation of microglia DEGs, plotted and filtered as in e. Black outlines mark the 3 displayed signalling terms that reach FDR < 0.05.
g, Locked macrophage genes: log2 fold change (POS / NEG) in all cells.
h, Locked microglia genes, plotted as in g.
